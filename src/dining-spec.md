# Dining area and furniture specification

## Product behavior

Dining is an authored floor-plan area with a fixed-size furniture layout. The default dining footprint is 8 m wide, 3 m high, and 6 m deep. Its layout origin is the polygon area centroid when that point is inside the polygon; for concave shapes where the centroid falls outside, the model selects the sampled interior point with greatest boundary clearance. The authored polygon remains the boundary of the dining region.

Tables and chairs keep real physical dimensions when the user resizes the area. Resizing affects fit and capacity, and must never stretch a chair or table. The default dining table is 1.2 × 0.8 × 0.75 m. A standard chair is 0.5 × 0.85 × 0.5 m with a 0.45 m seat, 0.42 m seat depth, 0.4 m back, and an 8° forward backrest tilt. Bar stools use a 0.75 m seat and a 0.3 m footrest. Height is the middle dimension in inventory metadata; geometry renders in world Y.

Supported chair finishes/styles are plastic stackable, wooden cafe, bistro wire, banquet padded, and bar stool. Layout presets are grid-four, grid-six, rows-banquet, round-six, round-eight, communal-long-tables, and cafe-bistro. Cafe-bistro uses two-seat 0.75 m square high tables at 1.05 m height. Banquet and communal tables are long fixed-size table segments; round presets use round tops, radial seats, and staggered triangular-lattice candidate positions. Stable IDs are derived from table and seat order.

## Layout and safety model

The model accepts a simple authored polygon and emits tables, chair placements, aisle polygons, and auxiliary placements. It uses the shared polygon primitives (`polygonContains`, point-in-polygon and polygon bounds) for concave-safe containment checks. It does not reuse parking vehicle layout, stall allocation, or circulation decisions.

The clearance targets are a 2 m ingress aisle connected to the selected entrance edge (the shortest polygon edge is the fallback), at least 1.5 m between tables, and at least 1 m between occupied chair backs. The current aisle is one trunk that ends 0.7 m before the polygon centroid to keep a connection pocket clear; branch routing to every seat is not yet generated. Seat approach patches are 0.3 m deep and start 0.3 m behind the chair blocker at floor level (Y=0). An empty chair blocker reaches 0.2 m under the tabletop; an occupied blocker extends about 0.4 m farther behind the chair. The deterministic seed introduces at most ±5° yaw and ±0.04 m radial jitter. Occupancy is a fraction in [0, 1] and each seat's occupied state is deterministic for a fixed seed.

The sample claim of six tables and 24 seats is a request, not an entitlement. Capacity is the number of chairs in placements that fit the fixed furniture and worst-case occupied clearance envelopes inside the authored polygon. This keeps chair IDs, positions, and capacity stable as occupancy changes; only the seated-avatar manifest and current blocker state change. The model emits warnings when it cannot fit the requested count or when the 2 m aisle cannot be kept clear. It does not report a requested seat count as achieved capacity. These clearances are planning heuristics, not formal ergonomic, accessibility, fire-code, or egress certification.

## Persisted customization

Dining-specific fields on `PlanObject` are optional to preserve old saved layouts. New-area defaults are exposed through `diningObjectDefaults()`: grid-four, plastic-stackable, seed 42, empty occupancy, no centerpiece, no tablecloth, no shade, no boundary treatment, no host, no bus tub, and a request for six tables. Standalone chairs use optional `chairStyle`; new chair inventory objects default to plastic-stackable, and `applyStandaloneChairStyle()` updates physical dimensions while preserving placement scale. Centerpieces are none, vase with flowers, candle lantern, or table number. Shade choices are none, umbrella, and pergola. Boundary treatment choices are none, planter, and rope. Optional tablecloth, host, and bus-tub flags add a cloth treatment or helper props. Planter and rope props omit the selected dining entrance/service edge; without one, the layout chooses the shortest edge, leaving an opening at least 1.2 m wide.

The optional vendor-link datum stores a vendor reference ID and selected service-edge index on the dining area. `diningVendorServiceWindow(object)` resolves it into a 4 m inward buffer polygon and reports whether that buffer fits the boundary. It is consumed by an editor/store adapter that owns selected placement; the layout engine does not own marketplace transaction state.

## Renderer contract

`diningLayout(object)` from `model/dining.ts` is the source of truth for placement and capacity. It returns a manifest with an interior origin, placed tables, each table's chairs, aisle polygons, auxiliary props, seated-avatar placements, actual capacity, requested capacity, and warnings. Renderers must use those placements directly rather than recalculate seat counts or scatter furniture independently. Table, chair, blocker, approach, target, and prop footprints are floor-plan coordinates in the dining object's local plan space. `chair.target` and `chair.approachPoint` identify the safe arrival point 0.3 m behind the chair blocker at floor level; `chair.facingTarget` identifies the table center for avatar orientation. Chair yaw is a Three.js world-Y rotation for model-local +Z forward. `diningAnchorPoint(object, 'seat', chairId)` returns the arrival point. `diningAnchorPoint(object, 'host', undefined, entrancePoint)` returns an inward-shifted host target when the editor supplies the real entrance anchor; without it, it falls back to the helper placement. Aisle polygons and chair approach polygons feed route/navigation tools; `DiningLayout.warnings` should remain visible to editors.

Chair model requirements for the renderer are stable silhouette and footprint across LODs, with simplified seat/back/leg geometry at distance. High detail may add chair joints, upholstery, wire back pattern, and material accents; far detail should retain seat/back mass and color only. Centerpiece and shade geometry may simplify first. Empty chair blockers remain under the tabletop while occupied chairs include the pulled-back envelope.
