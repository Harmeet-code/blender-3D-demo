## Repeatable device gate

1. Record the integrated-GPU laptop model, CPU/GPU, browser version and power state. Current host is an i5-12400F desktop with NVIDIA GT 710 and Chrome 154; it does not satisfy the laptop designation.
2. Build with `bunx vite build --base /expo/`; serve with `bunx vite preview --host 127.0.0.1 --port 4173 --base /expo/`.
3. Use 1280×720 and `http://127.0.0.1:4173/expo/?asset-stress=1&quality=low`. Verify forty booths, active F1, DPR 1, shadows 0 and opaque glass. Close other rendering tabs and avoid concurrent builds.
4. Open Asset diagnostics and run the 60-second camera tour. The script warms up for 10 seconds, then samples 60 seconds. Save the visible JSON, including browser/GPU, active floors, calls/triangles, bytes, texture estimates, average FPS and p95.
5. Require average FPS ≥30 and p95 ≤33.3 ms, main calls ≤100, normal calls ≤150, visible triangles ≤200,000, texture estimate ≤64 MiB and initial GLB transfers ≤8 MiB. Repeat after adjustments if any limit fails.
6. Complete tasks 1.4/13.2 and archive only after this device gate passes. A higher quality 60 FPS goal remains a measured target.

## Ownership and activation decisions

Floor switching is atomic: current-floor resources unmount before destination resources become active. There is no animated transition; the transition's zero-duration handoff still respects the two-floor ceiling. Dollhouse renders both floor assemblies while pausing walkthrough physics. The floor cache retains at most two metadata sets; inactive floor instance buffers/colliders are immediately disposed, not retained on the GPU. Common compiled geometry/materials remain under viewer ownership until the last viewer lease ends. Branding overlays own and dispose only their plane, material and texture.

Retry invalidates only failed GLB URLs and their failed boundaries. Successful instances keep their resources. Geometry rollback changes rendering without changing room IDs, selected booth or cart.
